# The Who Exactly? research method

## Fundamental question

For the product decision in front of us: **who already does this job, what have they actually tried, what currently works, and what would we need to observe before inviting them into an early group?**

## Six steps inside one specialist Bot

These are **steps**, deliberately not named plays. The Early Access Playbook has its own six canonical plays.

1. **Define the work:** actor, trigger, actual action, desired result, environment, current alternative and exclusion conditions. Separate facts from assumptions.
2. **Search where the work appears:** public posts with firsthand verbs, reported tasks, comparisons, unsuccessful attempts and successful existing methods. Use only actual available search tools.
3. **Check the person and episode:** retrieve original post and relevant context; label firsthand, secondhand, hypothetical, promotional or unknown; link and date source.
4. **Challenge the match:** explicit non-fits, satisfied incumbent users, different setups and rival explanations. Do not boost the list with vaguely related influencers.
5. **Prepare the invitation:** relevant, consent-first invitation based on what the person really said. Review, don't auto-send.
6. **Challenge the next group:** same underlying job, different relevant conditions. Stop when research no longer supports a useful decision.

## Epistemic ladder

1. Source actually retrieved.
2. Author publicly reported an episode.
3. Episode's relevance was interpreted and checked.
4. Founder contacted person (explicitly recorded, not inferred).
5. Person opted in voluntarily.
6. Actual product use was observed in a task.
7. Repeat value was independently measured.

Never collapse two levels into one. When presenting an inferred fit, explicitly say what further evidence would establish it.

## What good looks like

A useful output gives a founder enough evidence to answer **which people are worth interviewing or watching do the job**. It may be three highly qualified people, one strong counterexample, or a carefully explained no-results conclusion. It is *not* necessarily a long ranked list.

## Sources and modes

- Public research: immediate, source-backed, read-only and bounded by coverage.
- Personal-X research: specific X plugin authorization when a question actually requires it; only scopes supported by live tools.
- Bounded-input: evaluate provided evidence with explicit no-fetch labeling when search isn't possible.
- Persistent research: only call something a historical change when the record was actually saved earlier and comparable observations exist.
